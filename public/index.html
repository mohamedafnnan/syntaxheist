01 / the brief

Make the thing you wish already existed.

SyntaxHeist is a 24-hour open technology and innovation hackathon by IEEE FISAT Student Branch. Show up with a question, a sketch, or a half-working idea. The clock is there to help you make the next version.

The best idea in the room might still be a sketch.

Teams of students will spend one unbroken day turning problems into prototypes. Build a tool, a system, an experience, or a question made visible. There is no prescribed stack and no single right answer.

Come to learn in public, find collaborators, and ship a version that is more honest than the one you imagined alone.

Bring your own laptop, your sharpest question, and the willingness to show the messy middle.

04 / the 24-hour log

A little structure. A lot of room.

The schedule gives the day a pulse without telling your project what to become.

10:00 / 12:00

Open the room

Check-in, team formation, challenge drop, and a short briefing to get everyone pointed in the same direction.

12:00 / 20:00

Make the first version

Research, sketch, break, rebuild. Mentors circulate and teams move from “what if?” to “look.”

20:00 / 06:00

Stay with the problem

An overnight build block for the decisions that only happen when the easy answer is gone.

06:00 / 08:00

Polish the proof

Demo prep, final checks, and a clear story about what you made and why it matters.

08:00 / 10:00

Show the room

Final presentations, judging, people’s pick, and a closing circle before the clock runs out.

The small print

Questions before the heist?

Who can participate?

Students and recent graduates who want to build are welcome. You can arrive with a team or use the opening session to find people with a complementary skill set.

How many people can be on a team?

Teams of 2–4 are the sweet spot. A solo build is welcome too; use the team member field on the form to tell us what you are looking for.

Do I need to know a particular technology?

No. SyntaxHeist is open technology by design. Bring the tools that help you answer the question, not the tools that look impressive on paper.

What should I bring?

Your laptop, charger, student ID, a water bottle, and a willingness to explain your work as it changes. More event logistics will be shared after registration.

Where can I ask a question?

Write to ieeefisat@fisat.ac.in and include “SyntaxHeist” in the subject line.
